Tracing How the Brain Builds Language

The LingoBrain Lab uses MRI and EEG integrated with behavioral measures to study how bilinguals and clinical populations with language disorders process and produce sentences and discourse, and the neural mechanisms underlying these processes.

Explore our research Meet the lab
What we study

From neural signal to language comprehension and production

Our work sits at the intersection of neuroimaging and language science — combining brain data with computational tools to study how sentences and discourse are built in real communication.

Sentence processing in bilinguals

How bilinguals and second-language learners process complex sentence structure, and how this shapes neural organization for language.

Discourse & clinical populations

Discourse production following stroke, in schizotypy, and across other clinical presentations.

Child language development

Early markers of language delay in late-talking children, and building automatic speech recognition (ASR) models that better recognize the speech of children with language impairment.

Our approach

MRI, EEG, and deep learning

EEG
MRI
Deep Learning

We collect structural, functional, and diffusion MRI as well as EEG data during naturalistic and task-based communication. Analytic approaches are selected according to the aims of each study: some employ standard MRI and EEG methods, such as lesion-symptom mapping and time-frequency analysis, while others incorporate machine-learning techniques to characterize sentence- and discourse-level features.

This lets us ask questions that are hard to answer with behavioral data alone: how second-language learners process complex sentence structure, how discourse breaks down under schizotypal traits, or how automatic speech recognition models can be improved to better recognize the speech of children with language impairment.
