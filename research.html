Research

How We Study Language in the Brain

Our research centers on sentence and discourse processing — the level at which meaning is built — studied across bilinguals, clinical populations, and children.

Thread 01

Sentence processing in bilinguals & L2 learners

We examine how bilinguals and second-language learners process complex sentence structures — including relative clauses and wh-dependencies — using EEG time-frequency analysis (beta- and gamma-band oscillations) to track the timing and resource demands of parsing in a non-native language.

Thread 02

Discourse production

We study how people construct and express meaning across sentences and turns — from narrative generation to referential and inferential language — including how discourse production is affected by working memory load and schizotypal traits.

Thread 03

Child language development

We investigate early language development in late-talking children through speech act analysis. A current project builds and evaluates automatic speech recognition (ASR) models on children's speech, including children with language impairment, to improve ASR accuracy for these clinical pediatric populations.

Thread 04

Clinical & neurogenic language

We study language recovery after stroke, using longitudinal structural imaging and lesion-symptom mapping to identify neural correlates of verbal fluency and language outcomes.

Software & pipelines

What we run our analyses with

Neuroimaging

Structural, functional, and diffusion MRI, EEG, and lesion-symptom mapping, analyzed with tools including SPM, FSL, FreeSurfer, MNE, and AFNI/ANTs-based pipelines — using standard analytic approaches for each modality.

Speech & Language Analysis

Where appropriate, we use machine learning to analyze sentence- and discourse-level features, including speech act analysis in children's language and automatic speech recognition (ASR) models trained on children's speech to improve recognition accuracy for children with language impairment.

National Academies · PI

Identifying Language Biomarkers to Flag Prodromal Cognitive Decline in At-Risk and Underserved Aging Populations

Funded by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine as a subaward from a parent grant held by Indiana University, this project investigates language-based biomarkers that may signal early, prodromal cognitive decline in at-risk and underserved aging populations.
